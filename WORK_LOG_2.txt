SECTION 2 : DEFECTS FOUND BY MEASUREMENT (not by reading)
================================================================================

DEFECT 1 - FALSE DOCUMENTATION CLAIM, MODULE A  (severity: high, it misleads)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
FOUND   : segmentation_notes.txt and README.md both state "Chosen for the
          pipeline: watershed, because it is the only method here that both finds
          the few real boxes and stays fast, and it is the one that separates
          touching boxes via distance-transform markers."
REALITY : watershed_segmentation() is NOT the downstream detector.
          production_detections() - the function pipeline.py and
          kalman_tracker.py actually call - uses Otsu + morphological open/close +
          findContours(RETR_EXTERNAL). It never calls watershed at all.
IMPACT  : the notes name a method the pipeline does not use, and attribute
          touching-box separation to it, which the measurement below shows is
          false. A reviewer who read the notes and then read the code would find
          the code does not match its own documentation.

DEFECT 2 - WATERSHED MARKER GENERATION HAS A REAL BUG  (severity: medium)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
FOUND   : watershed_segmentation() computes markers as
              threshold = max(2.0, distance.max() * 0.45)
          over the WHOLE FRAME, from one distance transform of the whole binary
          foreground.
WHY     : on any frame where a dark region touches the frame border, the distance
          transform of that region grows toward the border and its maximum lands in
          a corner, far larger than the inradius of a real box. Measured on a
          640x360 frame with a belt strip 640x88 at the bottom: belt dist max
          = 90.0 px, the two boxes' inradius = 63.0 px. One global threshold of
          0.45 x 90.0 = 40.5 then sits above neither box's usable marker scale, so
          a small box and a huge border-touching region are forced to share a
          threshold that suits neither.
STATUS  : investigated, NOT changed. See 2.2 for why the obvious fix is worse.

DEFECT 3 - MODULE D's CORE DEMONSTRATION WAS INVISIBLE  (severity: high)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
FOUND   : the bundled synthetic clip has pixel-exact box edges, so
          production_detections() returns a measurement with zero noise. With a
          zero-noise measurement the Kalman posterior equals the measurement, so
          printed predicted / measured / corrected values are all identical and
          the filter appears to do nothing.
MEASURED: at --measurement-noise 1.5 the three stages visibly differ, e.g.
            frame 2  predict (111.50, 175.95)
                     measure (110.82, 174.01)
                     correct (111.25, 175.24)
          The brief's premise is "noisy position measurements", so the noise had
          to be representable to demonstrate anything at all.

DEFECT 4 - THE BRIEF'S TWO HEADLINE QUESTIONS WERE UNANSWERABLE  (severity: high)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Module B asks which of the four camera models is most accurate. measure_box.py
had no way to know, because nothing compared the estimates against truth.
Module E asks which method is robust to lighting vs rotation. Only rotation was
ever exercised.
Both are now measurable. Neither number existed before.

EXPERIMENT 1 - DOES WATERSHED SEPARATE TOUCHING BOXES?

METHOD   Built synthetic frames with known box geometry and scored every method

LAYOUTS  2 touching with a vertical offset (truth 2)
         3 touching with a vertical offset (truth 3)
         2 separate with a 20 px gap (truth 2)

RESULTS  layout: 2 touching, y offset (truth 2)
            method            det/frame  matched  missed  spurious   ms/f
            split_merge            8.40        0      10        42   15.7
            watershed              2.00        0      10        10   13.0
            felzenszwalb           4.00       10       0        10  426.6
            mean_shift            30.00        0      10       150 3059.6
            production(otsu)       2.00        0      10        10    2.0

         layout: 3 touching, y offset (truth 3)
            split_merge           11.40        4      11        53   13.8
            watershed              2.00        0      15        10   14.3
            felzenszwalb           4.00       15       0         5  221.5
            mean_shift            30.00        6       9       144 2210.2
            production(otsu)       2.00        0      15        10    2.1

         layout: 2 separate, 20 px gap (truth 2)
            split_merge            9.00       10       0        35   12.3
            watershed              3.00       10       0         5   12.5
            felzenszwalb           5.00       10       0        15  214.0
            mean_shift            30.00        5       5       145 2260.2
            production(otsu)       3.00       10       0         5    2.0

CONCLUSION (this is the brief's required discussion, now backed by numbers):
  Felzenszwalb is the ONLY method of the four that separates touching boxes -
  10/10 and 15/15 recall - because the concave step where two boxes meet is a
  low-gradient edge that the region-adjacency graph's minimum-cut criterion
  refuses to merge, while the box interiors are each internally coherent.
  Watershed and Otsu+RETR_EXTERNAL BOTH merge the touching pair (0 recall). Mean
  shift is unusable: it hits the 30-box cap on every single frame.
  On genuinely separate boxes watershed and Otsu both reach 10/10.
  So the honest answer is the opposite of what the project's notes claimed.

EXPERIMENT 2 - CAN THE WATERSHED MARKER BUG BE FIXED CHEAPLY?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
TRIED   (a) per-component distance threshold (fixes DEFECT 2)
        (b) distance-transform local maxima as markers
        (c) erosion-split cores: erode each blob by the smallest radius that
            breaks it apart, use the pieces as markers
        (d) combinations of the above

BUGS I INTRODUCED AND FIXED WHILE PROTOTYPING (recorded because they are the
classic failure modes of this code):
  - foreground markers numbered from 1 collide with cv2.watershed's reserved
    background label 1, so every marker was silently erased. Markers must start
    at 2 (0 = unknown, 1 = background).
  - background must be the area OUTSIDE the objects, i.e.
    dilate(binary == 0). Marking "everything without a marker" as background
    turns the unclaimed object interior into background and the watershed
    returns only the marker cores.
  - erosion over the full frame for 15 radii costs 239 ms/frame; it has to run
    inside each component's bounding box.

RESULT  variant              layout                     det/frame  matched    ms/f
        current watershed    2 touching, y offset          2.00      0/10    17.7
        per-component ws     2 touching, y offset          2.00      0/10    92.6
        production(otsu)     2 touching, y offset          2.00      0/10     2.8
        current watershed    2 separate, 20 px gap         3.00     10/10    16.4
        per-component ws     2 separate, 20 px gap         3.00      0/10   149.4
        production(otsu)     2 separate, 20 px gap         3.00     10/10     2.6

DECISION REJECT the rewrite. It is 5x to 9x SLOWER and it made the separate-box
        case WORSE (0/10 against the existing 10/10), because markers that are
        eroded cores leave the reported box short of the true edge. Keeping the
        existing detector is the correct engineering call, and the measured
        finding above is the honest deliverable for this module.

EXPERIMENT 3 - WHY IS MODULE E INSENSITIVE TO LIGHTING?  (this one overturned me)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
I first wrote the Module E robustness note asserting the eigenbox is the most
lighting-sensitive classifier, because PCA on raw pixels "ought" to be. The
measurement said otherwise:
            method                       upright   rotated 15   relit
            alignment                       91.7%         -        -
            eigenbox_pca                    91.7%         -        -
            hu_moments                      50.0%      50.0%     50.0%
            eigenbox_pca_rotated_15deg        -        58.3%       -
            eigenbox_pca_relit               -           -       91.7%
            hu_moments_relit                 -           -       58.3%
            ensemble_vote                   91.7%         -        -
            ensemble_vote_rotated_15deg       -        75.0%       -
            ensemble_vote_relit             -           -       91.7%
The eigenbox is UNCHANGED under relighting. I was about to ship a wrong note, so
I measured why:
            raw crop mean/std                   160.18    92.63
            relit crop mean/std                 110.78    74.02
            raw vs relit L1 distance              49.40
            after equalizeHist, prepared L1        0.00729
            raw vs rotated L1                      7.55
            prepared vs rotated L1                 0.14947
prepare_crop() calls cv2.equalizeHist, and equalisation is invariant to a
monotone remap of intensity - which is exactly what a gain and a gamma are. So
the 49.4 grey-level lighting shift collapses to 0.007, while the 15 deg rotation
still moves the prepared vector by 0.149, twenty times more, because no
equalisation can undo a rotation. Lighting robustness comes from the shared
preprocessing, not from any classifier. The note now says that, with the numbers.

SECTION 6 : DECISIONS TAKEN, WITH REASONS  (so they are not relitigated)
================================================================================
1. The production detector stays Otsu + findContours. The watershed rewrite was
   measured 5-9x slower AND less accurate on the separate-box case. Correctness
   of the running pipeline beat a more elegant segmentation.
2. The Module E robustness note was rewritten after the measurement contradicted
   my first draft. The lesson generalises: on this project, assert nothing that
   has not been measured. The existing WORK_LOG is explicit about an earlier
   "140x" headline that turned out to be an artefact of broken scoring.
3. The UI is dependency-free. requirements.txt pins upper bounds for
   reproducibility; a dashboard is not worth a framework that can break the
   install on a lab machine.
4. Module B's recommendation is printed from a ranking, not from prose. "Which
   camera model is most accurate" depends on the capture, so the tool scores all
   four against a supplied truth and lets the numbers choose.
5. Synthetic fixtures remain clearly labelled as smoke-test fixtures. The brief
   requires real footage for the final submission and that is still outstanding;
   nothing in this pass changes that.
6. Three UI bugs were found by curling the running server, not by reading it.
   NaN-in-JSON in particular cannot be seen by inspection and would have shipped
   a dashboard that silently failed on every response.
